Ten years ago, my job was to look at welds and decide whether they would hold.

I was a qualified Welding and Coating Inspector in South Africa. It's careful, technical work. You learn to look closely, ask why, and never sign off on something you haven't verified. I'm still proud of that training. But I knew it wasn't the future I wanted.

In 2013, I built my first WordPress site. It wasn't good. But something clicked: here was a door that anyone could walk through. No degree required, no permission needed, no gatekeeper checking where you came from. Just the open web, and whatever you were willing to teach yourself.

## The long middle

Nobody tells you about the long middle of a career change: the years where you're not the old thing anymore but not yet the new thing either.

I taught myself design and development and freelanced as Travis Lima Design. I took small projects and treated them like inspections: look closely, ask why, don't ship what you haven't verified. Eventually that work led me to Paid Memberships Pro, where I spent years in support, made tutorial videos, ran webinars, and learned what it actually takes for someone to build a business online, because I watched thousands of people try.

Support, it turns out, is the best education in ecommerce that exists. Every ticket is a real person with a real business hitting a real wall. You learn patterns fast.

## The bet pays off

Today I lead customer success conversations at WooCommerce, working with merchants doing millions in revenue. I've spoken at WordCamp Europe and WordCamp US. I've sat across from businesses processing over a million subscription orders a year. Businesses that, like me, started from nothing.

That's the part I can't get over: the same platform that changed my life is changing theirs.

## If you're standing where I stood

If you're in a career that doesn't fit, and you're wondering whether the open web is a real path: it is. Not an easy one. But a real one. Three things I'd tell 2013 me:

1. **Start before you're ready.** My first site was bad. It still changed everything.
2. **Support work is not a stepping stone, it's an accelerator.** Nothing teaches you a product, or an industry, faster.
3. **Your old skills come with you.** I still inspect things for a living. The welds just look different now.

The door is open. Walk through it.
